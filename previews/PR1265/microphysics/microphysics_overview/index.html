Microphysics

In atmospheric modeling, "microphysics" largely overlaps with "cloud physics" and concerns the formation, development, and precipitation of clouds. More generally, "microphysics" encompasses panoply of physical processes associated with (i) the conversion of water between vapor, liquid, and ice phases, and (ii) the interaction and transformation of "cloud particles", which include droplets, ice particles, and aerosols. For example: microphysical processes include droplet nucleation on aerosol particles; liquid freezing and vapor solidification; chemical and humidity-based transformations of aerosol particles; the agglomeration of ice particles into snowflakes; collisions and breakup of falling droplets (rain); collision between ice particles and droplets or wholesale freezing of droplets to form hail (e.g. Straka (2009)).

Breeze microphysics is under active development. Today, Breeze provides warm-phase and mixed-phase saturation adjustment, the bulk schemes of CloudMicrophysics.jl, and the Predicted Particle Properties (P3) mixed-phase scheme (see its theory and usage pages). Breeze aims to eventually provide a wide range of microphysical models, ranging from simple warm-phase saturation adjustment, to the bulk schemes provided by the Climate Modeling Alliance's CloudMicrophysics.jl, to superdroplet schemes, to spectral bin schemes that include a spectrum of droplet sizes, ice particle shapes and aerosol types.

Advection of microphysical fields

Each microphysics scheme chooses the default advection scheme for its own prognostic fields and for the moisture density it evolves, through default_microphysics_advection. For every scheme in Breeze (saturation adjustment, the one- and two-moment bulk schemes, Kessler, and P3) these fields use a bounds-preserving WENO scheme (order 5, or order 3 on grids with a horizontal halo of 3) with bounds (0, ∞), combined with an AdaptiveVerticallyImplicitDiscretization(cfl = 1/4) that treats the part of vertical transport, including sedimentation, above a Courant number of 1/4 implicitly. The bounds limiter keeps fields non-negative only up to a Courant number of 5/18 per forward-Euler substep, which is why the explicit part is capped at 1/4. Fast-sedimenting species such as rain therefore stay non-negative and stable at time steps where explicit centered advection would fail. P3's prognostic vapor excess, which is negative in subsaturated air, is the exception and uses the model-wide default.

Other scalars (the thermodynamic variable, user tracers) and momentum keep the model-wide default, Centered(order = 2), and so do all scalars when microphysics = nothing. A single advection or scalar_advection scheme passed to AtmosphereModel applies to every scalar, and an entry of a scalar_advection NamedTuple overrides the default for that field:

model = AtmosphereModel(grid; microphysics, scalar_advection = (; ρqʳ = Centered(order = 2)))

Bounded WENO with implicit vertical transport costs more per time step than Centered(order = 2): a wider stencil, a limiter evaluated each stage, and a tridiagonal solve per column and field.
