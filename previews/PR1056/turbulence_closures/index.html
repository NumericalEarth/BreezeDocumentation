Turbulence closures

A turbulence closure supplies the subgrid fluxes of momentum and of every tracer — the transport carried by motions the grid does not resolve. Breeze's closures are Oceananigans AbstractScalarDiffusivitys, so they compose with the rest of the model through the same diffusivity interface as Oceananigans' own. A closure may be either three-dimensional, as a subgrid-scale (or subfilter-scale) model for large-eddy simulation; or vertical only, commonly referred to as a planetary boundary-layer scheme.

Other available turbulence closures

In addition to the TKEBasedTurbulenceClosure described here, Breeze also inherits Oceananigans' own turbulence closures.

Prognostic-TKE eddy diffusivity

TKEBasedTurbulenceClosure is a vertical eddy-diffusivity closure with one prognostic equation, for the subgrid turbulent kinetic energy $e$, in the spirit of CATKE (Wagner et al. 2025). It is an empirical closure: the eddy diffusivities of momentum, scalars and turbulent kinetic energy are the products of a turbulent velocity $\sqrt{e}$ and a mixing length for each,

\[K^u = ℓ^u \sqrt{e}, \qquad K^c = ℓ^c \sqrt{e}, \qquad K^e = ℓ^e \sqrt{e},\]

where each mixing length is a stability function times one primary length $ℓ$,

\[ℓ^u = S^u ℓ, \qquad ℓ^c = S^c ℓ, \qquad ℓ^e = S^e ℓ,\]

and the dissipation of turbulent kinetic energy follows the same pattern with a dissipation length $ℓ^D = ℓ / S^D$,

\[ε = \frac{e^{3/2}}{ℓ^D} = S^D \frac{e^{3/2}}{ℓ}.\]

The turbulent kinetic energy obeys

\[∂_t (ρ e) + ∇ ⋅ (ρ 𝐮 e) = ∂_z (ρ K^e ∂_z e) + ρ (P + B - ε), \qquad P = K^u S², \qquad B = -K^c N²,\]

with shear production $P$ from the squared vertical shear $S² = (∂_z u)² + (∂_z v)²$, the buoyancy flux $B$ from the squared buoyancy frequency $N² = g \, ∂_z \ln θᵥ$, the dissipation $ε$, and transport. The density $ρ e$ is the tracer ρe, which the closure adds to the model's tracers; it is advected and vertically diffused like every other scalar, and the closure applies the local terms $P + B - ε$.

See the single-column boundary layer example for the closure in stable, neutral and convective boundary layers.

The mixing length

The primary mixing length (AbstractMixingLength) is built from the wall length $ℂˢ z$ and the buoyancy penetration depth $ℓᵇ = \sqrt{e} / N$, which is infinite where $N² ≤ 0$. Every formulation gives $ℓ = ℂˢ z$ in a neutral surface layer and $ℓ = ℓᵇ$ in uniform stratification; they differ elsewhere:

  • LocalMinimumMixingLength: $ℓ = \min(ℂˢ z, ℓᵇ)$, after Deardorff (1980). In an elevated layer with $N² ≤ 0$, such as a saturated cloud layer, only the distance to the ground bounds $ℓ$, so the diffusivity can jump by orders of magnitude.
  • GradientLimitedMixingLength (default): $ℓ(z) = \min(ℂˢ z, \min_{z′}[ℓᵇ(z′) + ℂⁱ |z - z′|])$, which also bounds $ℓ$ by the distance to nearby stratification, growing at slope $ℂⁱ$. The envelope before capping at the surface length is stored in closure_fields.ℓˢ.
  • IntegralMixingLength: the parcel length of Bougeault and Lacarrère (1989), $ℓ = ℂˢ \min(ℓ↑, ℓ↓)$, where $ℓ↑$ and $ℓ↓$ are the distances a parcel with energy $e / (2 ℂˢ²)$ travels up and down before its accumulated buoyancy deficit consumes that energy. It penetrates inversions more than the gradient-limited length, at the cost of a state-dependent walk per face.

The mixing length is stored in closure_fields.ℓᵐ. The standalone constructors default to $ℂˢ = 1.316$ (GradientLimitedMixingLength(ℂˢ=c) also sets $ℂⁱ = c$); the default closure uses the calibrated $ℂˢ ≈ 0.788$ and $ℂⁱ ≈ 1.78$.

Static stability

$N²$ enters the stratification length, the buoyancy flux, and $Ri$. It is diagnosed once per stage at cell interfaces and stored in closure_fields.N², according to the closure's static_stability. DryStaticStability uses $g \, ∂_z \ln θᵨ$, with $θᵨ$ the density potential temperature. MoistStaticStability, the default, uses the dry value where subsaturated and, where saturated, the saturated buoyancy frequency of Durran and Klemp (1982),

\[N²_s = g \left[ \frac{1 + ℒ rˢ / (Rᵈ T)}{1 + ϵ ℒ² rˢ / (cᵖᵈ Rᵈ T²)} \left( ∂_z \ln θ + \frac{ℒ}{cᵖᵈ T} ∂_z rˢ \right) - ∂_z rᵗ \right],\]

where $rˢ$ and $rᵗ$ are the saturation and total-water mixing ratios, $ϵ = Rᵈ / Rᵛ$, and $ℒ$ is the latent heat, taken from the microphysics' phase equilibrium. Saturation is tested using the local moist density and excludes precipitation, while the loading term includes all condensate. $N²_s$ nearly vanishes on a saturated adiabat, so mixing is not shut off inside stratocumulus.

Stability functions

The default is RationalStabilityFunction, $S(Ri) = C⁰ + (C^± - C⁰) x / (1 + x)$ with $x = |Ri| / Ri^±$, which is continuous but kinked at neutral. The Richardson number is $Ri = N² / (S² + S²ᵐⁱⁿ)$, with the closure's minimum_shear_squared $S²ᵐⁱⁿ = 10⁻¹⁰ \, \mathrm{s⁻²}$ by default. The floor keeps $Ri$ finite and continuous where the shear vanishes, as in uniform or absent wind: there $N² / S²$ would jump between the stable and unstable limits with the sign of a rounding-level $N²$. With the floor the functions pass from neutral to their limits over $|N²| ∼ Ri^± S²ᵐⁱⁿ$, and where $S² ≫ S²ᵐⁱⁿ$ $Ri$ differs from $N² / S²$ by the relative amount $S²ᵐⁱⁿ / S²$ at most. Their defaults, $ℂᵘ = 0.149$, $ℂᶜ = 0.201$, $ℂᵉ = 0.298$, $ℂᴰ = 0.388$, are the coefficients of Nakanishi and Niino (2009), giving $κ = 0.40$, $Pr = 0.74$ and $Ri^\dagger = 0.25$.

Numerics

The diffusivities are computed at the cell interfaces where the fluxes live, from the stored mixing length and $\sqrt{e}$ reconstructed from the cell centers and floored at minimum_tke. The numerics of the TKE equation follow CATKE. The sinks — dissipation and the negative part of the buoyancy flux, as the rate $-Lᵉ = Sᴰ \sqrt{e} / ℓ + |B⁻| / \max(e, eᵐⁱⁿ)$ with $eᵐⁱⁿ$ the minimum_tke (so that the buoyancy sink is continuous at $e = eᵐⁱⁿ$ and falls linearly with $e$ below it), and the damping of $e$ that advection drives negative, at the rate $1/τ$ — enter the vertically implicit tridiagonal solve of every time-step stage together with the vertical diffusion of $e$, so that $e$ stays positive for any time step. The sources — shear production and the positive part of the buoyancy flux — enter the tendency of the same stage. Shear production uses the current stage's velocities in both factors of $Kᵘ S²$; its spatial reconstruction retains CATKE's grid-spacing weights. Under an explicit time discretization the sinks enter the tendency as well.
