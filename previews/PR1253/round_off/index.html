Round-off in reduced precision

Each stage of a Runge–Kutta time stepper ends with a sum $u^{(m)} = u^{(0)} + δ$. In Float32 that sum is rounded to the nearest representable number, and any increment $δ$ smaller than half the spacing between floats near $u^{(0)}$ (half an ulp) is lost. For $ρθ ≈ 100$–$600$, half an ulp is $4×10⁻⁶$ to $3×10⁻⁵$, about the size of one stage's increment from radiative cooling of $1.5$ K/day at $Δt = 2.5$ s. The loss has the same sign at every step, so it is a bias, not noise: the model stops warming or cooling while the radiation scheme keeps reporting the heating rate (issue #1122).

The round-off treatment of the stage updates is selectable through timestepper_kwargs, for both the SSPRungeKutta3 stepper of the anelastic core and the AcousticRungeKutta3 stepper of the compressible core:

  • RoundToNearest (default): IEEE rounding, bitwise identical to the original stage update and free.
  • StochasticRounding: each stage is evaluated in increment form, $u^{(m)} = u^{(0)} + α \left[(u^{(m-1)} - u^{(0)}) + Δt \, G\right]$, and the final sum is rounded to one of its two neighboring floats, choosing each with probability proportional to its closeness to the exact sum. On CPUs a Float32 sum is computed in Float64, random bits are added below the Float32 significand and the result is truncated (WidenedSum); on GPUs, where Float64 arithmetic is slow or unavailable, and for Float64, the rounding error is computed with TwoSum (TwoSum). Every stage is then exact in expectation, at the cost of an ulp-sized random walk. The random numbers are a counter-based hash of the seed, the iteration, the stage, the field and the grid indices, so there is no random state: runs are bitwise reproducible and restart reproducibly from checkpoints. It needs no extra memory.
using Oceananigans, Breezegrid = RectilinearGrid(Float32; size=(8, 8, 8), extent=(1000, 1000, 1000))model = AtmosphereModel(grid; timestepper_kwargs = (; rounding = StochasticRounding(seed = 1)))model.timestepper.rounding# outputStochasticRounding(seed=1, summation=WidenedSum())

On the compressible core, the Wicker–Skamarock stages of AcousticRungeKutta3 all restart from $Uⁿ$, so only the third stage's rounding reaches $Uⁿ⁺¹$; that stage's recovery of the acoustic prognostics and its scalar updates are rounded with the chosen treatment. The vertically-implicit solves, the pressure correction, and the acoustic perturbation updates stay round-to-nearest.

Stochastic rounding works in any precision; in Float64 it is supported but rarely useful, since increments are resolved. The default leaves Float64 and Float32 runs unchanged.

In a radiative-convective equilibrium LES on the anelastic core (liquid-ice potential temperature), the energy residual over days 10–20 was:

PrecisionEnergy residual (W/m²)
Float64+1.77
Float32 with a Float64 pressure solver−1.61
Float32 with StochasticRounding+1.78

On the split-explicit compressible core, Float32 with StochasticRounding gave −2.56 W/m² against −2.59 W/m² in Float64. Stochastic rounding cost about 20% in wall time in those runs.
